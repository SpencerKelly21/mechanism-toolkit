Equations used
  • Inverting: G = −Rf/Rin, input impedance Rin. Non-inverting: G = 1 + Rf/Rg. Follower: G = 1.
  • Summing: Vout = −Rf·(V1/R1 + V2/R2). Difference (matched resistors): Vout = (Rf/R1)·(V2 − V1).
  • Closed-loop bandwidth of the amplifiers: f(−3 dB) = GBW / noise gain, where the noise gain is 1 + Rf/R(seen at the inverting node). Gain falls as G / √(1 + (f/f₋₃dB)²).
  • Integrator: Vout = −(1/RC)∫Vin dt, so a DC input ramps the output at −Vin/RC. Unity-gain frequency 1/(2πRC). An ideal integrator drifts to a rail; real ones add a large resistor across C.
  • Differentiator: Vout = −RC·dVin/dt, gain 2πfRC for a sine of frequency f. High-frequency gain is unbounded, so practical circuits add a small series resistor.
  • Inverting low-pass: DC gain −Rf/Rin, fc = 1/(2π·Rf·Cf), |H| = (Rf/Rin)/√(1 + (f/fc)²). Inverting high-pass: fc = 1/(2π·Rin·Cin), |H| = (Rf/Rin)·(f/fc)/√(1 + (f/fc)²).
  • Output limit: the output cannot exceed ±Vs. Real parts fall short of the rails by a volt or more unless they are rail-to-rail.
