Equations used
  • Each magnet is two magnetic poles, +q at the north end and −q at the south end, a distance L apart along the angle a. Its moment is m = q·L.
  • Field of one pole at displacement d: B = q·d / (|d|² + ε²)^(3/2), with ε = 0.15 to soften the singularity at the pole. The total field is the vector sum over every pole. Far from a magnet this reduces to the usual dipole field.
  • Field lines start on a small ring around every north pole and follow B in steps of 0.06 (midpoint rule) until they leave the view or reach a south pole. Strength is |B| on a log scale from 0.03 to 30.
  • Force on pole i from pole j of another magnet: F = qi·qj·d / (|d|² + ε²)^(3/2) with d = ri − rj. Like poles push apart, unlike poles pull together. The net force on a magnet sums this over its two poles and all poles of the other magnets.
  • Torque about the magnet's centre c: τ = Σ (ri − c) × Fi, positive counter-clockwise.
  • Limits: an idealised pole model of fixed-strength magnets, with no iron, demagnetisation or time variation. Forces become unreliable when poles of different magnets come within about 0.5 units.
