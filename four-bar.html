Equations used
  • Crank pin A = r2·(cosθ2, sinθ2) with the crank pivot at the origin and the follower pivot at (d, 0). Joint B is the intersection of a circle of radius r3 about A and a circle of radius r4 about the follower pivot: with D = |A − O4|, a = (r3² − r4² + D²) / 2D and h = √(r3² − a²), B = A + a·û ± h·n̂. The ± is the assembly mode. B exists only when |r3 − r4| ≤ D ≤ r3 + r4.
  • Coupler point C = A + p·û + q·n̂, where û runs from A to B and n̂ is its left normal.
  • Grashof test: with shortest link s, longest l and the other two p, q, the linkage has a fully rotating link if s + l < p + q. The shortest link decides the type: crank gives a crank-rocker, ground a drag link, coupler a double-rocker, follower a rocker-crank. If s + l > p + q it is a triple-rocker.
  • Transmission angle μ is the angle between coupler and follower at B (the smaller of μ and 180° − μ). Keep it above about 40° for good force transfer.
  • Velocity ratio from the loop equation, with ω2 = 1 and vA = (−Ay, Ax): ω3 = (vAx(Bx − d) + By·vAy) / Δ and ω4 = ((By − Ay)·vAy + (Bx − Ax)·vAx) / Δ, with Δ = (By − Ay)(Bx − d) − By(Bx − Ax).
  • Ideal torque ratio T4 / T2 = ω2 / ω4 (power in equals power out, no friction or inertia). It grows without limit near the toggle positions.
  • Coupler point speed: vC = vA + ω3·ẑ × (C − A), per unit of crank speed.
  • Quick-return ratio for a crank-rocker: Q = (crank angle swept one way between the follower limits) ÷ (the other way).
